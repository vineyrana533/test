Travelling from New York to Los Angeles: what you need to know

  • Depart from LaGuardia Airport (LGA) in Queens; the only commercial passenger terminal at LGA serves all domestic flights.
  • Arrive at Los Angeles International Airport (LAX), the primary gateway to Southern California, located in Westchester.
  • Average non‑stop flight time is about 5 hours 45 minutes, covering roughly 3 965 km.
  • New York operates on Eastern Time (UTC‑5/‑4) while Los Angeles is on Pacific Time (UTC‑8/‑7), a three‑hour difference.
  • The busiest pairing on this corridor is LGA ↔ LAX, with most airlines scheduling two‑hourly departures during peak periods.
  • For an international‑style security regime, aim to be at LGA at least two hours before departure, especially in summer and holiday weeks.

How to find the cheapest flights from New York to Los Angeles

  • Book 6‑8 weeks ahead for the best balance of price and seat availability; last‑minute fares can surge 30‑50 %.
  • January and February are historically the cheapest months, with lower demand after the holiday rush.
  • Set a price‑alert on a comparison site and monitor for drops of 5‑10 % before you purchase.
  • Use the “cheapest” filter first, then sort by shortest travel time to avoid overly long connections.

Flying from New York: LaGuardia Airport

  • JetBlue, Delta, American and United all operate non‑stop services from LGA to LAX.
  • Common one‑stop hubs include Chicago O’Hare (ORD), Dallas/Fort Worth (DFW) and Denver (DEN).
  • LGA sits about 8 km (5 mi) from Manhattan; driving takes 20‑30 minutes depending on traffic.
  • Public transport: the Q70 SBS bus to the 7‑train then a 30‑minute ride to the airport, or the M60 SBS to the subway, totals roughly 45‑60 minutes.

Arriving in Los Angeles: Los Angeles International Airport

  • LAX is approximately 30 km (19 mi) southwest of downtown Los Angeles; car journeys range from 30‑45 minutes.
  • The FlyAway Bus runs nonstop to Union Station in about 45 minutes and costs a flat fare.
  • Metro’s Green Line connects to the C Line (Airport Line) at Aviation/LAX station; a combined shuttle‑train trip takes roughly 60 minutes.
  • Ride‑share and rental‑car services are plentiful; traffic can add 15‑20 minutes during peak hours.

Best time to go to Los Angeles

  • July and August see the highest visitor numbers, driven by school holidays and warm weather.
  • February and early March are quieter, offering lower accommodation rates and milder crowds.
  • Summer temperatures average 24‑30 °C, while winter lows sit around 9‑14 °C; evenings can be cool year‑round.

More about Los Angeles

  • The Getty Center showcases European paintings, modern sculpture and striking gardens.
  • Hollywood’s Walk of Fame and the TCL Chinese Theatre mark the heart of the film industry.
  • Santa Monica Pier offers a classic boardwalk, an aquarium and a historic carousel.
  • The Arts District and Downtown’s Grand Central Market deliver a vibrant food‑scene and street art.

Explore more of California

  • San Diego lies about 190 km (120 mi) south of Los Angeles; its Balboa Park and Gaslamp Quarter are top attractions.
  • San Francisco is roughly 610 km (380 mi) north; the Golden Gate Bridge, Alcatraz and Fisherman’s Wharf draw millions.
  • Sacramento, the state capital, sits about 620 km (385 mi) northeast and features the historic Old Sacramento waterfront.

Which airports serve Los Angeles (LAX) and New York (LGA), and how far are they from the city centre?

What is the best time of year to visit Los Angeles, and what is the weather like?

The best time to visit Los Angeles is late spring (April‑June) and early autumn (September‑October), when daytime temperatures average 18‑26 °C, humidity is low and rainfall is minimal, providing ideal conditions for beaches, hiking and city sightseeing. Summer can be hotter, often exceeding 30 °C, while winter brings cooler evenings and occasional rain.
