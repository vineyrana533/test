Travelling from Los Angeles to Tokyo: what you need to know

  • Depart from Los Angeles International Airport (LAX) and arrive at Narita International Airport (NRT) for most inter‑continental services.
  • The average non‑stop flight time is 11‑12 hours, covering roughly 8 753 km.
  • Los Angeles is UTC‑8 (Pacific Time) while Tokyo is UTC+9 (Japan Standard Time), a 17‑hour time‑difference (16 hours when daylight‑saving is in effect in the US).
  • The LAX‑NRT pairing is the busiest Japan‑US corridor, with several daily services.
  • For an international departure, aim to be at the terminal at least two hours before take‑off, and a little earlier during peak travel periods such as Golden Week or the US holidays.

How to find the cheapest flights from Los Angeles to Tokyo

  • Prices are typically lowest when you book 8‑12 weeks ahead; last‑minute fares can jump 30‑50 % above the average.
  • January and February are historically the cheapest months, with lower demand after the New Year celebrations.
  • Set a price‑alert on a comparison site and let it notify you when the fare drops below your target.
  • Use the “cheapest” or “shortest‑duration” filter and then sort by price to see the best value for your dates.
  • Consider flying out of nearby airports such as John Wayne (SNA) or Ontario (ONT) if you have flexibility; they sometimes feed cheaper connections.

Flying from Los Angeles: Los Angeles International Airport

  • LAX handles all five airlines that operate non‑stop services to Tokyo and is located about 30 km southwest of downtown Los Angeles.
  • By car the journey takes 30‑45 minutes outside rush hour; the FlyAway® bus to LAX runs 45‑60 minutes from Union Station.
  • The Metro C Line connects to the airport via the Aviation/LAX station and a free shuttle, adding roughly 60‑70 minutes from central LA.
  • Common one‑stop hubs for connecting flights are Vancouver (YVR), Seoul‑Incheon (ICN) and Hong Kong (HKG), each adding 2‑4 hours of layover time.
  • LAX has a single passenger terminal complex split into nine concourses; follow signage for International Arrivals (Concourse B‑G).

Arriving in Tokyo: Narita International Airport

  • Narita (NRT) sits 60 km east of central Tokyo and is the primary gateway for long‑haul flights from North America.
  • The Narita Express (N’EX) reaches Tokyo Station in 50‑60 minutes; the Keisei Skyliner reaches Ueno in 40‑45 minutes.
  • By car or taxi the journey is 60‑90 minutes depending on traffic; airport limousine buses serve major hotels and stations in about 70‑90 minutes.
  • Once in the city, the extensive JR Yamanote loop and subway network provide easy access to districts such as Shinjuku, Shibuya and Akihabara.
  • Narita has two terminals (1 and 2) for international flights; follow the signs for “Immigration” and “Baggage Claim”.

Best time to go to Tokyo

  • April is the most popular month, coinciding with cherry‑blossom (sakura) viewing across the city.
  • January and July offer lower crowds and cheaper accommodation, though July can be hot and humid.
  • Summer (June‑August) temperatures range from 25 °C to 35 °C with high humidity; winter (December‑February) sees 2 °C to 10 °C, occasional light snow in the outskirts.
  • Golden Week (late April‑early May) and the New Year period see the highest demand and steepest prices.
  • Autumn (late September‑November) offers mild weather (15‑22 °C) and vibrant foliage, making it a balanced choice for comfort and cost.

More about Tokyo

  • Shibuya Crossing, often photographed as the world’s busiest intersection, is surrounded by shopping and nightlife.
  • The historic Senso‑ji Temple in Asakusa provides a glimpse of old Edo with its iconic Kaminarimon gate.
  • Meiji Shrine, set in a forested enclave near Harajuku, is a major cultural site and popular for traditional weddings.
  • Tsukiji Outer Market remains a top spot for fresh sushi and street food despite the wholesale market moving to Toyosu.
  • Akihabara’s electric town is famed for electronics, anime merchandise and themed cafés.

Explore more of Japan

  • Osaka lies about 400 km west of Tokyo; the Shinkansen reaches Osaka (Shin‑Osaka Station) in roughly 2.5 hours. Highlights include Osaka Castle and the vibrant Dotonbori district.
  • Kyoto is approximately 450 km southwest; a 2.5‑hour bullet‑train ride brings you to its UNESCO World Heritage sites such as Kinkaku‑ji (Golden Pavilion) and Fushimi Inari Taisha.
  • Hiroshima, around 800 km southwest, is a 4‑hour Shinkansen trip away and is known for the Peace Memorial Park and Miyajima Island’s Itsukushima Shrine.

Arriving in Tokyo: Haneda Airport

  • Haneda (HND) is only 14 km south of central Tokyo and handles many intra‑Asian and some trans‑Pacific services.
  • The Tokyo Monorail reaches Hamamatsucho Station in 15‑20 minutes; the Keikyu Airport Line connects to Shinagawa in about 20 minutes.
  • By car or taxi the airport is 20‑30 minutes from the city centre, depending on traffic.
  • Haneda’s Terminal 3 is dedicated to international arrivals, with clear signage for immigration and baggage claim.
  • For travellers prioritising a shorter ground‑transport time, Haneda often beats Narita, especially when staying in southern districts like Shibuya or Roppongi.

Finding cheap flights from Los Angeles to Tokyo: frequently asked questions

How do I find cheap flights from Los Angeles to Tokyo?

To find cheap flights from Los Angeles (LAX) to Tokyo (NRT) you should use flexible‑date searches, set price alerts on this page, and compare fares for departures on Tuesdays or Wednesdays. Checking both the main Narita airport and the secondary Haneda (HND) can reveal lower‑priced options. Booking 6‑8 weeks ahead, avoiding peak travel periods such as Golden Week in Japan, and being open to one‑stop itineraries with carriers like Air Canada or ANA often yields the best savings.

What is the cheapest way to fly from Los Angeles to Tokyo?

The cheapest way to fly from Los Angeles to Tokyo is to book a economy fare on a low‑cost carrier or a major airline’s discounted fare, travel in the off‑peak season, and be flexible with both departure and arrival airports. Using this page to compare fares for Narita (NRT) and Haneda (HND), selecting flights with a single stop in a North American hub, and booking 2‑3 months in advance typically produces the lowest overall cost.

When is the best time to book a flight from Los Angeles to Tokyo to get the lowest price?

The best time to book a flight from Los Angeles (LAX) to Tokyo (NRT) for the lowest price is about 7‑9 weeks before departure, with a secondary window 2‑3 months ahead for deep‑discount sales. Monitoring price alerts on this page and booking on Tuesdays or Wednesdays, when airlines often release fare updates, further improves the chance of securing a cheap ticket, especially for travel outside the Japanese cherry‑blossom and autumn‑leaf seasons.

What is the cheapest month or time of year to fly from Los Angeles to Tokyo?

The cheapest month to fly from Los Angeles to Tokyo is typically January or February, when demand is low after the New Year holidays and before the spring tourism surge. Late autumn, especially November, also offers competitive fares. Flying during these periods avoids the high‑price peaks of March (cherry‑blossom season) and July‑August (summer holidays), resulting in lower average ticket prices on this route.

How long is the flight from Los Angeles to Tokyo?

The non‑stop flight time between Los Angeles International Airport (LAX) and Narita International Airport (NRT) is usually 11 to 12 hours, covering a distance of approximately 8,753 kilometres. Factors such as wind direction, the specific aircraft type—often a Boeing 777 or Airbus A350—and the chosen flight path can cause slight variations, but most direct services fall within this time window.

Which airlines fly from Los Angeles to Tokyo?

Airlines that operate flights between Los Angeles (LAX) and Tokyo (NRT) include All‑Nippon Airways (ANA), Japan Airlines (JAL), American Airlines, United Airlines, Delta Air Lines, and Air Canada. These carriers offer both non‑stop and one‑stop services, with ANA and JAL providing the most frequent daily direct flights, while the North American legacy carriers often route through their respective hubs such as Vancouver or Seattle.

Are there direct (non‑stop) flights from Los Angeles to Tokyo?

Yes, there are direct non‑stop flights from Los Angeles International Airport (LAX) to Narita International Airport (NRT) operated by All‑Nippon Airways (ANA), Japan Airlines (JAL), American Airlines, United Airlines and Delta Air Lines. These services typically run several times per day, especially during peak travel periods, and provide a 11‑hour‑plus flight without intermediate stops, making them the fastest option between the two cities.

How much does a flight from Los Angeles to Tokyo usually cost and what drives the price?

A typical economy‑class ticket from Los Angeles (LAX) to Tokyo (NRT) costs between US$700 and US$1,200, with price variations driven by season, booking window, and demand. Peak periods such as cherry‑blossom season (late March‑early April) and Japanese holidays raise fares, while off‑peak months like January lower them. Airline competition, fuel prices, and the choice between a non‑stop versus one‑stop itinerary also influence the final price shown on this page.

What is the best day of the week and time of day to fly this route for a lower fare?

The cheapest day to depart from Los Angeles (LAX) to Tokyo (NRT) is usually Tuesday or Wednesday, and flights that leave early in the morning or late at night often have lower fares. Mid‑week departures avoid the weekend business‑travel premium, while off‑peak departure times reduce demand on the aircraft, resulting in price reductions that can be seen when you compare fares on this page.

Which airports serve Tokyo and Los Angeles, and how far are they from the city centre?

Los Angeles is served by Los Angeles International Airport (LAX), located about 30 kilometres southwest of downtown Los Angeles, while Tokyo is served primarily by Narita International Airport (NRT), situated roughly 60 kilometres east of central Tokyo. Both airports have a single main terminal for international traffic, and each offers rail, bus and taxi connections that reach the city centre within 45‑90 minutes depending on traffic and service chosen.

How many flights operate between Los Angeles and Tokyo, and what is the distance?

On an average day, more than 15 scheduled flights operate between Los Angeles (LAX) and Tokyo (NRT), including both non‑stop and one‑stop services offered by multiple carriers. The great‑circle distance between the two airports is approximately 8,753 kilometres, which translates to an 11‑hour‑plus flight for direct services and a slightly longer total travel time when connections are involved.

What is the best time of year to visit Tokyo, and what is the weather like?

The best time to visit Tokyo is during spring (late March to early May) and autumn (late September to November) when temperatures are mild, ranging from 12 °C to 22 °C, and humidity is moderate. Spring brings cherry‑blossom festivals, while autumn offers clear skies and colourful foliage. Summer can be hot and humid, often exceeding 30 °C, and the rainy season in June adds occasional downpours.

Which is the cheapest airline on this route, and what is the trade‑off?

Among the carriers serving Los Angeles to Tokyo, Air Canada often provides the lowest base fare, especially on one‑stop itineraries via Vancouver. The trade‑off is a longer total travel time, typically 2‑3 hours added for the connection, and potentially tighter baggage allowances compared with full‑service Japanese airlines such as ANA or JAL, which charge more but offer non‑stop flights and premium cabin service.

What major connecting hub is often used for flights between Los Angeles and Tokyo?

A common connecting hub for flights between Los Angeles (LAX) and Tokyo (NRT) is Vancouver International Airport (YVR), used by Air Canada and WestJet for one‑stop itineraries. Another frequent hub is Seattle‑Tacoma International Airport (SEA), served by Delta Air Lines and Alaska Airlines. These North‑west Pacific gateways provide convenient layovers and can lower the overall fare when direct options are fully booked or priced higher.
