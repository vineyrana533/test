Travelling from Newark to Santo Domingo: what you need to know

  • Depart from Newark Liberty International Airport (EWR), the only commercial airport serving Newark, with three terminals.
  • Arrive at Las Américas International Airport (SDQ), the main gateway to Santo Domingo, which has a single passenger terminal.
  • Average non‑stop flight time is roughly 4 hours 30 minutes.
  • Newark operates on Eastern Time (UTC‑5 in winter, UTC‑4 in summer) while Santo Domingo stays on Atlantic Standard Time (UTC‑4) year‑round, giving a one‑hour difference for most of the year.
  • The busiest airport‑to‑airport pairing on this route is EWR ↔ SDQ, with multiple daily services during peak season.
  • For an international departure, aim to be at the check‑in hall at least two hours before take‑off, and allow extra time during the December holiday period.

How to find the cheapest flights from Newark to Santo Domingo

  • Book 6‑8 weeks ahead for the best balance of price and seat availability; last‑minute fares rise sharply in December.
  • Historically, January and February are the cheapest months, with lower demand after the New Year celebrations.
  • Set up a price‑alert on a comparison site; the tool will email you when fares dip below your target.
  • Use the “cheapest” filter first, then sort by duration to spot good value on slightly longer itineraries that include a short layover.

Flying from Newark: Newark Liberty International Airport

  • United Airlines and American Airlines operate the only regular non‑stop services from EWR to SDQ; Delta Air Lines offers seasonal nonstop flights.
  • Common one‑stop connections include Panama City (Panama) via Copa Airlines, Toronto (Canada) via Air Canada, and Miami (USA) via American or Delta.
  • The airport sits about 24 km (15 miles) from downtown Newark; a car journey typically takes 30‑35 minutes depending on traffic.
  • Public transport: take the AirTrain to Newark Airport Rail Station, then NJ Transit’s Northeast Corridor or North Jersey Coast line – total travel time around 45 minutes to the city centre.

Arriving in Santo Domingo: Las Américas International Airport

  • SDQ is roughly 15 km (9 miles) east of the historic Colonial Zone; a taxi or rideshare usually reaches the centre in 20‑25 minutes.
  • Public options include the Santo Domingo Metro Line 2 (ends at the airport) and municipal bus routes; the metro ride to the city centre is about 15 minutes.
  • Ground‑transport fares are modest – a taxi from the terminal to the Colonial Zone costs around US $25‑$30, while a metro ticket is under US $1.
  • Once in the city, the Metro, public buses and affordable taxis make it easy to reach attractions without a rental car.

Best time to go to Santo Domingo

  • The most popular month is January, when many North Americans escape winter.
  • For quieter, cheaper visits, target June or early July before the peak summer school holidays.
  • Summer (June‑September) temperatures average 28‑32 °C with high humidity and occasional showers.
  • Winter (December‑February) is milder, ranging from 22‑27 °C, offering comfortable sightseeing weather.

More about Santo Domingo

  • The Colonial Zone, a UNESCO World Heritage site, showcases cobbled streets, the first cathedral of the Americas and the Alcázar de Colón.
  • The Malecón waterfront promenade offers ocean views, restaurants and lively nightlife.
  • National Botanical Garden provides a peaceful escape with native flora and walking trails.
  • The modern Distrito Nacional district houses museums, shopping malls and the bustling Plaza de la Cultura.

Explore more of the Dominican Republic

  • Punta Cana (≈ 210 km east of Santo Domingo) is famed for all‑inclusive resorts, Bávaro Beach and the Indigenous Eyes Ecological Park.
  • Santiago de los Caballeros (≈ 150 km north) is the country’s second‑largest city, known for the Monumento a los Héroes and nearby tobacco farms.
  • Puerto Plata (≈ 210 km north) offers historic Fort San Felipe, the Amber Museum and the cable‑car ride up Mount Isabel de Torres.

Which airports serve Santo Domingo and Newark, and how far are they from the city centre?

Newark is served by Newark Liberty International Airport (EWR), which has three terminals (A, B and C) and lies about 16 km (10 mi) from downtown Newark and roughly 24 km (15 mi) from Manhattan. Santo Domingo is served by Las Americas International Airport (SDQ), a single‑terminal facility located approximately 10 km (6 mi) east of the historic Zona Colonial and 12 km (7 mi) from the city centre. Both airports are well connected to their urban cores by taxis, rideshare services and public buses.

How many flights operate between Newark and Santo Domingo, and what is the distance?

On a typical week, there are about 12 to 15 scheduled flights between Newark Liberty International Airport (EWR) and Las Americas International Airport (SDQ), with JetBlue and Delta each operating two to three non‑stop services daily and other carriers offering one‑stop options. The great‑circle distance between the two airports is roughly 2,512 kilometres (1,562 miles), which translates to a four‑hour‑plus flight time. Seasonal variations can increase the frequency during the winter tourism peak.

What is the best time of year to visit Santo Domingo, and what is the weather like?

The best time to visit Santo Domingo is from December through April, when the island experiences its dry season with average daytime temperatures between 25 °C and 30 °C (77‑86 °F) and low humidity. Rainfall is minimal, and sunshine dominates, making it ideal for exploring the historic Zona Colonial and coastal attractions. The summer months of June to August are hotter and wetter, while September to November bring a higher chance of tropical showers.

Which is the cheapest airline on this route, and what is the trade‑off?

JetBlue Airways is generally the cheapest airline on the Newark‑Santo Domingo route, offering low base fares especially in the basic‑economy class. The trade‑off is a stricter baggage allowance—only one personal item and a small carry‑on are included, with checked bags incurring additional fees, and seat selection may cost extra. In contrast, legacy carriers such as Delta provide a higher fare but include a checked bag and more generous seat‑assignment options.

What is the most convenient way to get from Las Americas Airport to the historic Zona Colonial?

The most convenient way to travel from Las Americas International Airport (SDQ) to Santo Domingo’s historic Zona Colonial is by taking a licensed airport taxi or an Uber ride, which costs roughly US $15‑$20 and takes about 20‑25 minutes. Public buses (routes 4 and 15) are cheaper but slower, while a private shuttle can be pre‑booked for groups. All options provide easy access to the colonial centre, which lies approximately 10 km east of the airport.
