Overview of the route

The trans‑Atlantic journey from New York’s John F. Kennedy International Airport to London Heathrow is one of the most heavily trafficked international corridors. Passengers can expect a flight duration of around seven and a half hours, with the majority of airlines offering multiple daily departures. The route is served by a mix of legacy carriers and low‑cost operators, ensuring a range of service levels and price points. The flight path typically crosses the North Atlantic, following a great‑circle route that is both fuel‑efficient and time‑saving.

Travelers often choose this route for business, tourism or family visits, and the high frequency of flights provides flexibility in scheduling. Because the flight crosses the International Date Line, passengers will arrive in London a day earlier than their departure date, which can be a useful feature for time‑sensitive itineraries.

Getting to and from JFK

John F. Kennedy International Airport is located about 21 kilometres south of Manhattan. The airport is served by the AirTrain, which connects to the Long Island Rail Road and the New York City Subway. The AirTrain’s JFK‑LIRR station is a 15‑minute ride to Jamaica Station, from which the LIRR runs to Penn Station in 60 minutes. Alternatively, the AirTrain’s JFK‑Subway station links to the E and J lines, offering a 30‑minute journey to Manhattan.

For those arriving by car, the airport offers extensive parking facilities, including short‑term, long‑term and economy options. Taxis, rideshare services and private shuttles are also available from all terminals. The airport’s six terminals cater to a wide range of airlines, with dedicated lounges and a comprehensive retail and dining selection.

Heathrow and London access

London Heathrow Airport sits 12 kilometres west of central London and is served by Heathrow Express, the Elizabeth Line, the Piccadilly Line and various coach operators. The Heathrow Express offers a 15‑minute journey to Paddington, while the Elizabeth Line takes 45 minutes to reach the city centre. The Piccadilly Line provides a 45‑minute tube ride to Leicester Square.

Heathrow’s four terminals are connected by the free Heathrow Airport Shuttle, which operates 24/7. The airport also offers a range of car‑hire, taxi and rideshare services. For those preferring a quieter arrival point, Gatwick, Stansted or Luton airports are alternative options, each with its own rail and coach connections to central London.

Finding the cheapest fares

To secure the best price on the JFK‑LHR route, start by comparing fares across multiple platforms and set price alerts for the dates you intend to travel. Mid‑week departures, particularly on Tuesdays and Wednesdays, tend to be cheaper than weekend flights. Early‑morning or late‑evening flights often carry lower fares, as airlines aim to fill seats that are less attractive to leisure travellers.

Consider booking 6‑8 weeks in advance; this window typically balances price and availability. For those with flexible dates, searching a full month can reveal significant savings, especially during the low‑season months of January and February. Using frequent‑flyer miles or credit‑card points can also reduce the cash cost of a ticket.

Best time to travel

The most cost‑effective period for the trans‑Atlantic flight is between January and March, when demand from both business and leisure travellers is lower. Autumn, particularly September and October, also offers competitive fares while providing pleasant weather in London.

Summer months (June to August) see the highest ticket prices, driven by increased demand for holiday travel and business conferences. The winter holiday period (late December to early January) is the most expensive, with peak pricing for Christmas and New Year’s travel. If you prefer a quieter city, consider visiting in late spring or early autumn, when the weather is mild and the crowds thinner.

Exploring London

Once in London, the city’s extensive public transport network makes getting around straightforward. The Underground, buses, and overground trains cover the city and suburbs, while the Oyster card or contact‑less payment provides a convenient fare system. Taxis and rideshare services are also plentiful.

Key highlights include the historic Westminster area, home to the Houses of Parliament, Westminster Abbey and Buckingham Palace. The British Museum, the Tate Modern, and the Natural History Museum offer world‑class cultural experiences. For a taste of modern London, the Shard and the Sky Garden provide panoramic views, while the vibrant markets of Camden and Borough offer culinary delights.

Finding cheap flights from New York to London: frequently asked questions

How do I find cheap flights from New York to London?

To find cheap flights from New York (JFK) to London (LHR), search for flexible dates, book 6–8 weeks before departure, and compare fares across airlines such as British Airways, Virgin Atlantic, American, Delta, United, Air France, and Lufthansa. Use the date‑range feature on this page, set alerts, and consider flying on Tuesdays or Wednesdays when fares tend to be lower. Avoid peak travel periods like Christmas and summer, and look for early‑morning or late‑evening departures to reduce costs.

What is the cheapest way to fly from New York to London?

The cheapest way to fly from New York to London is to book a low‑fare ticket with airlines that offer competitive prices, such as British Airways or Virgin Atlantic, and to book 6–8 weeks in advance. Choosing a Tuesday or Wednesday departure and early‑morning or late‑evening flight times can lower fares. Avoid peak seasons and consider alternative routes with a short layover in a European hub if a direct flight is too expensive.

When is the best time to book a flight from New York to London to get the lowest price?

The best time to book a flight from New York to London is 6 to 8 weeks before the intended departure. Prices typically rise as the travel date approaches, especially during summer and holiday periods. Booking in late October to early March often yields lower fares, and monitoring price alerts on this page can help you spot sudden drops.

What is the cheapest month or time of year to fly from New York to London?

The cheapest months to fly from New York to London are usually late October through March, with January and February offering the lowest average fares. Mid‑winter flights avoid the holiday rush, while early spring (April to May) also sees moderate prices. Avoid July, August, and the Christmas period when demand and prices peak.

How long is the flight from New York to London?

A direct flight from New York (JFK) to London (LHR) takes approximately 7 hours and 30 minutes, covering a distance of about 5,540 kilometres. Flight times can vary by up to 15 minutes depending on wind and routing.

Which airlines fly from New York to London?

Direct airlines operating from New York (JFK) to London (LHR) include British Airways, Virgin Atlantic, American Airlines, Delta Air Lines, United Airlines, Air France, Lufthansa, and Emirates (via Dubai). Low‑cost carriers such as Norwegian previously offered flights but have ceased operations on this route.

Are there direct (non‑stop) flights from New York to London?

Yes, there are several non‑stop flights from New York (JFK) to London (LHR). British Airways, Virgin Atlantic, American Airlines, Delta, United, Air France, Lufthansa, and Emirates all operate direct services, typically departing twice or three times a day.

How much does a flight from New York to London usually cost / what drives the price?

A round‑trip fare from New York to London usually ranges from £200 to £600, depending on season, airline, and booking window. Prices rise during summer, Christmas, and major UK events. Low‑fare carriers and early‑morning or late‑evening flights tend to be cheaper. Baggage fees, seat selection, and travel insurance can add to the base fare.

What is the best day of the week and time of day to fly this route for a lower fare?

Flying on Tuesdays or Wednesdays typically yields the lowest fares for the New York‑London route. Early‑morning departures (before 6 am) or late‑evening arrivals (after 10 pm) often have reduced demand, which can lower prices. Mid‑day flights tend to be more expensive.

Which airports serve London (and New York), and how far are they from the city centre?

London is served by Heathrow Airport (LHR), located about 23 km west of central London. New York is served by John F. Kennedy International Airport (JFK), situated roughly 13 km southeast of Manhattan. Both airports have multiple terminals—Heathrow has four, JFK has six—and are connected to city centres by rail, bus, and taxi services.

How many flights operate between New York and London, and what is the distance?

Daily, there are roughly 30 non‑stop flights between New York (JFK) and London (LHR), operated by major carriers such as British Airways, Virgin Atlantic, American, Delta, United, Air France, Lufthansa, and Emirates. The great‑circle distance is approximately 5,540 km, making it one of the most heavily trafficked trans‑Atlantic routes.

What is the best time of year to visit London, and what is the weather like?

The best time to visit London is late spring (May) and early autumn (September), when temperatures range from 12 °C to 20 °C and rainfall is moderate. Summer (June‑August) can be hot and crowded, while winter (December‑February) is cooler, with temperatures around 2 °C to 8 °C and frequent rain. Spring offers mild weather and blooming parks.

Which is the cheapest airline on this route, and what is the trade‑off?

British Airways often provides the most competitive fares for the New York‑London route, especially when booked early. The trade‑off is that low‑cost carriers like Norwegian (now ceased) would have offered even cheaper tickets but with limited baggage allowance and longer layovers. With British Airways, you benefit from free checked baggage and a higher seat‑pitch, but fares may still be higher than a budget carrier with a stop‑over.

What is the best way to avoid long security lines at JFK?

To avoid long security lines at JFK, use TSA PreCheck or Global Entry, arrive at least two hours before departure, and choose a terminal with dedicated pre‑check lanes (Terminals 4, 5, or 6). Flying with airlines that offer priority security, such as American or Delta, can also reduce wait times.
