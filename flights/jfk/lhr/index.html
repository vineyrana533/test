Overview of the New York to London flight

The trans‑Atlantic hop from John F. Kennedy International Airport (JFK) to Heathrow Airport (LHR) covers roughly 5,540 kilometres and typically takes about seven hours on a direct flight.

Passengers will cross from the Eastern Standard Time zone to Greenwich Mean Time, meaning a 5‑hour time difference that can be managed with a full night’s rest on the plane. Arriving in London in the early afternoon local time is common, giving you a day to settle in.

Because the route is heavily trafficked, fares fluctuate widely. Prices tend to rise during the summer and around major holidays, while mid‑week departures often remain more affordable.

Getting to and from JFK

JFK is served by six terminals, with Terminals 4 and 5 handling most long‑haul flights to Europe. The AirTrain links the terminals to the Long Island Rail Road and the New York City Subway, offering a cost‑effective way to reach Manhattan or the boroughs.

Taxis and ride‑share services are readily available outside each terminal, but expect a surcharge for airport pickups. For those arriving by train, the AirTrain departs every 4–10 minutes, making it a convenient option for travellers on a tight schedule.

Arriving at Heathrow

Heathrow’s four terminals are dedicated to different airlines: Terminal 5 is the hub for British Airways, Terminal 4 hosts Virgin Atlantic, Terminal 2 is used by JetBlue and other carriers, and Terminal 3 handles low‑cost airlines such as Ryanair. The airport is well connected to central London via the Heathrow Express (15‑minute journey to Paddington), the Piccadilly Line of the Underground, and a network of buses.

Customs and immigration are located on the arrival level of each terminal, and the airport’s security procedures are standard for international flights.

Finding the cheapest fares

To secure the best price, start by comparing airlines that operate the route directly – British Airways, Virgin Atlantic, Delta, United, American and JetBlue – as well as low‑cost carriers that offer trans‑Atlantic services. Use fare‑comparison sites that allow you to filter by departure and arrival times, and set up price alerts for the New York‑London corridor.

Booking 2–4 months in advance often yields lower fares, and searching for flights that depart on Tuesday or Wednesday can reduce costs further. Avoid peak holiday periods and consider flying out of JFK rather than Newark for slightly better deals.

Best time to travel

Late spring (May and June) and early autumn (September and October) provide mild weather, fewer crowds, and moderate fares. Summer months (July and August) bring higher prices and larger crowds, especially around major events such as Wimbledon or the Proms.

Winter travel (December to February) offers the lowest fares but can be chilly and damp. If you prefer a quieter visit, aim for mid‑September, when the summer rush has ended but the weather remains pleasant.

Exploring London

London’s public transport network – the Underground, buses, and overground trains – makes it easy to reach major attractions from Heathrow. A single‑journey Oyster card or contact‑less payment gives you access to the Tube, buses and National Rail services.

Key sights include Buckingham Palace, the Tower of London, the British Museum, and the Houses of Parliament. The city’s vibrant neighbourhoods – Camden, Notting Hill, and Shoreditch – offer diverse dining and shopping experiences. For a day trip, consider the historic towns of Windsor or Oxford, both within an hour’s train journey.

Finding cheap flights from New York to London: frequently asked questions

How many airports are there in London?

London is served by five major airports: Heathrow (LHR) – 23 km west of the city centre, Gatwick (LGW) – 32 km south, Stansted (STN) – 64 km north‑east, Luton (LTN) – 45 km north‑west, and City (LCY) – 4 km east of the financial district. Southend (SEN) is a smaller, regional airport 63 km east. Heathrow is the busiest, handling the majority of international traffic, while Gatwick and Stansted offer more budget‑friendly options.

How much are return flights from New York to London?

Return fares on the New York‑London corridor typically range from around $350 to $900, depending on the airline, time of year and how far in advance you book. Prices rise during summer, Christmas and Easter, and fall in late autumn and early winter. Low‑cost carriers can offer the lowest fares, but they often charge extra for baggage, seat selection and onboard services.

How long does it take to fly from New York to London?

A direct flight from JFK to Heathrow usually takes about 7 hours and 15 minutes. Connecting flights, which are common when using carriers that route through hubs such as Dublin, Reykjavik or Frankfurt, add 2–4 hours of total travel time, depending on the layover duration.

Which airlines fly direct from New York to London?

Major carriers that operate nonstop services include British Airways, Virgin Atlantic, Delta Air Lines, United Airlines, American Airlines and JetBlue. Low‑cost options such as Norwegian (when it operated trans‑Atlantic routes) and some seasonal services also offered direct flights, though availability can vary by season.

How many flights are there a week from New York to London?

The route is one of the busiest trans‑Atlantic corridors, with dozens of daily departures from JFK to Heathrow. In a typical week you can expect at least 30–40 nonstop flights, plus a comparable number of connecting services that use European hubs.

When is the cheapest time to fly from New York to London?

The lowest fares are usually found in September, October, January and February. Mid‑week departures, especially on Tuesday or Wednesday, are often cheaper than weekend flights. Early morning or late‑night departures can also offer savings, as can flights that depart from JFK rather than Newark.

Which is the cheapest airport to fly into near London?

Heathrow is the most convenient for international flights but can be more expensive. Gatwick and Stansted often offer lower fares, especially from carriers that use those airports as hubs for budget services. If you are flexible, compare the total cost of flight plus ground transport to the city centre.

Which upcoming month is usually the cheapest to fly on this route?

September is typically the most affordable month for New York‑London flights. Fares tend to rise again in October as the summer rush returns, so booking in early September can secure the best price.

Can I get a direct flight from New York to London?

Yes, direct flights are available from JFK to Heathrow on a daily basis with several airlines. Direct services are also offered from Newark (EWR) to Heathrow, though the flight time is slightly longer due to the greater distance from the airport to the city.

How can I get the best flight deal on this route?

Set price alerts on comparison sites, book 2–4 months ahead, and keep an eye on fare sales from carriers such as British Airways, Virgin Atlantic, Delta and low‑cost operators. Use flexible dates, avoid peak holiday periods, and consider flying into alternate airports like Gatwick or Stansted if you are willing to add a short train ride to central London.

When is the best time to book flights to London?

The sweet spot for booking is 2–4 months before departure. This window often captures early‑bird discounts and avoids the price spikes that occur during the summer and around major holidays. For last‑minute travel, monitor airline promotions and use fare‑alert tools to catch sudden drops.

What is the weather like in London, and what is the best season to visit?

London has a temperate maritime climate: mild summers (average 18–22 °C) and cool winters (average 2–7 °C). Rainfall is spread evenly throughout the year. The best seasons for sightseeing are late spring (May–June) and early autumn (September–October) when the weather is pleasant and tourist crowds are moderate.

Which is the cheapest airline to fly this route with, and what is the trade‑off?

Low‑cost carriers such as JetBlue and, historically, Norwegian offered the lowest base fares. The trade‑off is that they charge extra for checked baggage, seat selection, and onboard meals. Comfort and convenience are also reduced compared to full‑service airlines, but for budget‑conscious travellers the savings can be substantial.

What is the most popular layover hub for flights between New York and London?

Aer Lingus often routes its flights through Dublin, making it a common layover for passengers on the New York‑London corridor who choose that carrier. Icelandair uses Reykjavik as a hub for its trans‑Atlantic services, while Lufthansa and British Airways typically use Frankfurt or Amsterdam for connecting flights. These hubs offer convenient transfer times and a range of onward connections within Europe.
