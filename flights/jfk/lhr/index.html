Overview of the New York to London flight

The trans‑Atlantic hop from John F. Kennedy International Airport to Heathrow covers roughly 5,540 kilometres and takes around seven hours on a non‑stop flight. The route is one of the busiest in the world, with a mix of full‑service carriers and low‑cost options. Seasonal demand means that prices can swing from the low‑hundred pound range in winter to the mid‑hundreds in summer.

Passengers often choose to fly mid‑week for the quietest flights and the best fares. The time of day is less important than the day of the week and the month of travel.

Getting to and from JFK Airport

JFK is served by four main terminals, connected by the AirTrain that links the airport to the Long Island Rail Road and the New York City Subway. The AirTrain to Jamaica Station takes about 20 minutes and offers a direct connection to the E, J, and Z lines. From Manhattan, a taxi costs roughly £70–£80 plus a 50‑pound airport surcharge, while rideshare services provide a cheaper alternative.

For those arriving by car, the airport offers extensive parking facilities, including short‑term, long‑term, and economy lots. The closest public transport hub is the Jamaica Station, about 12 kilometres from the airport.

Heathrow Airport and city access

Heathrow, the main gateway into London, sits about 12 kilometres west of the city centre and is served by four terminals. The Heathrow Express offers a 15‑minute journey to Paddington station for a flat fare, while the Piccadilly Line of the Underground takes around 50 minutes and is the cheapest option. Buses, taxis, and private hire vehicles provide direct links to most boroughs.

Parking is available at the airport, but the cost can be high. For visitors on a budget, the Underground or a shared taxi is recommended.

Finding the cheapest fares

To secure the best deal, compare fares across the main carriers – British Airways, Virgin Atlantic, American, Delta, United, Emirates, Qatar, and Etihad – using a price‑comparison engine. Flexibility with dates and departure times can unlock significant savings; mid‑week flights are usually cheaper than weekend departures. Setting up fare alerts and booking 6–8 weeks in advance for the off‑peak season, or 3–4 months ahead for the summer, can also help.

Low‑cost carriers such as Norwegian and WOW Air (when operating) occasionally offer competitive rates, but they may involve extra stops or less convenient airports.

Best time of year to travel

Winter months (January and February) offer the lowest fares and fewer crowds, though the weather is cooler and wetter. Spring (March to May) brings mild temperatures and blooming parks, making it ideal for sightseeing. Summer (June to August) sees higher prices and larger crowds, but the days are longest and the weather is warm. Autumn (September to November) provides comfortable temperatures and fewer tourists, with the benefit of autumn foliage.

The peak tourist season is July and August, when both flights and accommodation are at their most expensive.

Exploring London after arrival

London’s public transport network – the Underground, overground trains, buses, and river services – makes city‑wide exploration straightforward. Key sights include Buckingham Palace, the Tower of London, the British Museum, and the Tate Modern. The city’s cycling lanes and bike‑share schemes offer a more active way to see the sights. For a day trip, consider visiting Windsor Castle, Oxford, or the historic town of Bath.

Accommodation ranges from luxury hotels in Mayfair to budget hostels in East London; booking in advance during peak season can secure better rates.

Finding cheap flights from New York to London: frequently asked questions

How do I find cheap flights from New York to London?

To find cheap flights from New York (JFK) to London (LHR), search with flexible dates, book 1–3 months ahead, and avoid peak summer and Christmas periods. Compare fares on this page, noting that mid‑week departures, especially Tuesdays and Wednesdays, often carry lower prices. Consider nearby airports such as Newark (EWR) or LaGuardia (LGA) for additional options, and use the fare‑calendar to spot the cheapest days within your travel window.

What is the cheapest way to fly from New York to London?

The cheapest way to fly from New York (JFK) to London (LHR) is to book a low‑cost carrier such as British Airways’ partner on the trans‑Atlantic route, or a discount fare from airlines that offer competitive rates like Virgin Atlantic, American Airlines, Delta, or United. These airlines often provide lower base fares but charge extra for checked baggage, seat selection and in‑flight meals. Booking via this page and setting price alerts can help you spot the lowest available price before the fare increases.

When is the best time to book a flight from New York to London to get the lowest price?

The best time to book a flight from New York (JFK) to London (LHR) for the lowest price is usually 6–8 weeks before departure, with fares peaking during the first month after the flight date and again in the last two weeks of the month. Booking earlier than 6 weeks can secure lower fares, especially during the low‑season months of January to March or September to October, when demand is lower and airlines offer promotional discounts.

What is the cheapest month or time of year to fly from New York to London?

The cheapest months to fly from New York (JFK) to London (LHR) are typically January, February, and September, when demand is lower and airlines run off‑peak promotions. Late March and early October also offer competitive fares, but prices rise during the peak summer months of June to August and around Christmas and New Year. Booking in the low‑season months can reduce the base fare by up to 20 % compared with peak periods.

How long is the flight from New York to London?

The flight duration from New York (JFK) to London (LHR) is approximately 7 hours and 15 minutes on a direct, non‑stop flight operated by airlines such as British Airways, Virgin Atlantic, Delta, or United. Wind conditions and the chosen flight path can add or subtract a few minutes, but the average time remains around 7 hours, making it one of the longest trans‑Atlantic routes in the United States.

Which airlines fly from New York to London?

Airlines that operate flights from New York (JFK) to London (LHR) include British Airways, Virgin Atlantic, Delta Air Lines, United Airlines, American Airlines, Air France, Lufthansa, and Scandinavian carriers such as SAS. These carriers offer a mix of non‑stop and one‑stop services, with British Airways and Virgin Atlantic providing the most frequent direct flights, while airlines like Air France and Lufthansa often route through Paris or Frankfurt for connecting flights.

Are there direct (non‑stop) flights from New York to London?

Yes, there are direct, non‑stop flights from New York (JFK) to London (LHR). Airlines such as British Airways, Virgin Atlantic, Delta Air Lines, United Airlines, and American Airlines operate daily non‑stop services, typically taking around 7 hours and 15 minutes. These flights depart from JFK’s Terminal 4 or 5 and arrive at Heathrow’s Terminal 4 or 5, offering convenient connections to the city centre.

How much does a flight from New York to London usually cost and what drives the price?

A typical round‑trip fare from New York (JFK) to London (LHR) ranges from £350 to £700 for economy class, depending on the airline, booking window, and season. Prices are driven by fuel costs, airport fees, and demand cycles; peak summer and holiday periods push fares above £700, while low‑season months can see fares drop to the lower end of the range. Additional charges for checked baggage, seat selection, and in‑flight meals also influence the final cost.

What is the best day of the week and time of day to fly this route for a lower fare?

Flying from New York (JFK) to London (LHR) on a Tuesday or Wednesday tends to yield the lowest fares, as airlines often release discounted tickets mid‑week to fill seats. Early‑morning departures around 6 a.m. or late‑afternoon arrivals near 6 p.m. local London time also reduce competition, resulting in slightly cheaper fares compared to weekend or late‑night flights.

Which airports serve London and New York, and how far are they from the city centre?

London is served by Heathrow Airport (LHR), located about 23 km (14 miles) west of central London, and Gatwick Airport (LGW), roughly 45 km (28 miles) south‑east of the capital. New York’s main international hub is John F. Kennedy International Airport (JFK), situated 21 km (13 miles) south‑east of Manhattan, with LaGuardia (LGA) 12 km (7 miles) and Newark Liberty (EWR) 42 km (26 miles) also offering trans‑Atlantic services.

How many flights operate between New York and London, and what is the distance?

There are typically 30–35 daily flights between New York (JFK) and London (LHR), operated by airlines such as British Airways, Virgin Atlantic, Delta, United, and American. The great‑circle distance between the two airports is approximately 5,540 km (3,450 miles), making it one of the longest trans‑Atlantic routes from the United States.

What is the best time of year to visit London, and what is the weather like?

The best time to visit London is late spring (late April to early June) and early autumn (September to early October), when temperatures average 15–20 °C (59–68 °F) and rainfall is moderate. These periods avoid the peak summer heat and holiday crowds, offering pleasant walking weather and lower accommodation costs compared to July and August.

Which is the cheapest airline on this route, and what is the trade‑off?

On the New York (JFK) to London (LHR) route, the most economical carriers are typically British Airways’ partner on the trans‑Atlantic route and airlines such as Virgin Atlantic, which offer lower base fares. The trade‑off is that these tickets often exclude checked baggage, seat selection, and in‑flight meals, and the flight time may be slightly longer if a stop‑over is required. For a fully inclusive experience, higher‑priced airlines like Delta or United include more amenities.

What is the best way to transfer from Heathrow to central London?

The most convenient way to travel from Heathrow Airport (LHR) to central London is the Heathrow Express, which takes 15 minutes to Paddington and costs around £25. Alternatively, the London Underground Piccadilly Line offers a cheaper £6.50 fare and takes about 45 minutes. For budget travellers, the National Express coach to Victoria Coach Station runs every 15 minutes and costs roughly £10, providing a cost‑effective link to the city centre.
