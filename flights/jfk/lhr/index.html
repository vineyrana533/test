Overview of flying the route

Flying from New York to London is one of the most popular transatlantic routes, with a flight distance of 5,540 km.

The journey typically takes just over seven hours and offers a range of options from full‑service carriers to low‑cost operators.

Whether you prefer a morning departure to start your London day or a late‑evening flight to arrive after dinner, the schedule is dense enough to accommodate most itineraries.

The route is served by several airlines that offer non‑stop services, making it convenient for both business and leisure travellers.

Airports serving New York and getting to/from them

John F. Kennedy International Airport (JFK) is the main gateway for transatlantic flights out of New York. It sits on the eastern edge of Queens, about 21 km from Manhattan, and is served by the AirTrain, the 7 train, and several bus routes.

Most airlines that fly to London depart from Terminal 4 or Terminal 5, where check‑in and security are streamlined for international traffic.

For passengers arriving at JFK, the AirTrain connects to the Long Island Railway and the subway, making it easy to reach the city centre within 45 minutes.

Airports serving London and getting to/from them

Heathrow Airport (LHR) is the busiest airport in the UK and the primary hub for New York‑bound flights. Located 23 km west of central London, it is served by the Heathrow Express, the Piccadilly line, and a network of coaches.

Terminals 2, 3, 4 and 5 handle most international arrivals, with dedicated lounges and fast‑track services for premium passengers.

After landing, a 15‑minute train ride to Paddington gives access to the Circle, Hammersmith & City and District lines, while taxis and rideshare services are plentiful for a more direct transfer.

How to find the cheapest fares on this route

Use fare‑comparison engines that specialise in transatlantic routes, and set price alerts for the dates you plan to travel.

Look for flights that depart early in the morning or late at night, as these times are often cheaper.

Booking six to eight weeks ahead usually yields the best prices, but keep an eye on airline promotions that arise after the schedule is released.

If you’re flexible with your arrival airport, Gatwick can offer lower fares, though you’ll need to add a short train or bus journey into the city.

Best time of year to travel and what to expect

London’s climate is mild, with temperatures ranging from 7 °C in January to 22 °C in July. The city is busiest during the summer months and around Christmas, when flights and accommodation prices peak.

Late spring (May–June) and early autumn (September) provide pleasant weather, fewer crowds and lower prices. During these shoulder periods, you’ll find more flight options and better hotel rates, making it an ideal time for sightseeing without the summer rush.

About London city (getting around, a few highlights)

Once in central London, the Underground network covers most tourist sites, and the Oyster card or contactless payment offers convenient travel.

A hop‑on hop‑off bus tour gives a quick overview of landmarks such as Buckingham Palace, the Houses of Parliament and the Tower of London.

For a cultural day, visit the British Museum, the Tate Modern and the West End theatres. The city’s parks – Hyde Park, Regent’s Park and Richmond Park – provide green spaces for a relaxed break from the urban bustle.

Finding cheap flights from New York to London: frequently asked questions

How long is the flight from New York to London?

The non‑stop flight from JFK to Heathrow takes around 7 hours and 30 minutes, covering the 5,540‑km trans‑Atlantic distance. Most carriers schedule a single‑stopover flight of roughly 9–10 hours if you choose a connecting airline. The exact duration can vary by wind patterns and the specific aircraft, but 7½ hours is the typical on‑time average for a direct flight.

Which airlines fly from New York to London?

The main carriers operating non‑stop services between JFK and Heathrow are British Airways, Virgin Atlantic, American Airlines, Delta Air Lines, United Airlines, Air France, and Lufthansa. In addition, carriers such as Air Canada and KLM offer flights with a short layover in their hubs. These airlines provide a mix of premium and economy cabins, with frequent‑flyer benefits for regular travellers.

Are there direct (non‑stop) flights from New York to London?

Yes, all of the airlines listed above operate direct flights from JFK to Heathrow. The route is one of the busiest trans‑Atlantic corridors, with several non‑stop services each day. Some carriers also offer direct flights to London Gatwick, but Heathrow is the primary hub for most long‑haul flights from New York. These direct services depart throughout the day, with early‑morning, midday, and late‑evening options.

How much does a flight from New York to London cost / what drives the price?

Economy fares typically range from £300 to £800 when booked in advance, with premium cabins starting around £1,200. Prices rise during peak travel periods such as Christmas, New Year, and summer holidays, and fall in the shoulder months of late September to early November. Factors such as airline brand, baggage allowance, seat selection, and booking window all influence the final cost.

What is the cheapest month/time to fly from New York to London?

The most economical period is usually late September through early November, when demand drops after the summer peak. February and March also offer competitive fares, especially if you avoid the Easter holiday. Booking mid‑week and flying during off‑peak hours can further reduce costs, as airlines often lower prices for less popular travel times.

What is the best day of the week and time of day to fly this route for a lower fare?

Tuesday and Wednesday mornings are generally the cheapest days, as airlines release fare discounts early in the week and there is less weekend demand. Late‑night departures, around 10 pm to midnight, also tend to be cheaper, while early‑morning flights can be slightly more expensive due to higher demand for business travellers.

How far in advance should I book flights from New York to London?

For the best fares, book 6 to 8 weeks before departure during off‑peak seasons. During peak periods, such as summer or Christmas, it is wise to book 3 to 4 months ahead to secure a reasonable price. Checking fare alerts and monitoring price trends can help you time your purchase for the lowest possible cost.

Which airports serve London (and New York), and how far are they from the city centre?

In New York, JFK is the primary international gateway, located about 20 km (12 mi) south of Manhattan and accessible via the AirTrain, subway, or taxi. In London, Heathrow is the main trans‑Atlantic hub, situated roughly 23 km (14 mi) west of central London and served by the Heathrow Express, Piccadilly Line, and numerous coach services. Gatwick, about 46 km (29 mi) south, is a secondary option for some airlines.

How many flights operate between New York and London?

The JFK–Heathrow corridor sees around 40 to 50 non‑stop flights daily, with additional services that include a short layover in hubs such as Paris, Frankfurt, or Dublin. The frequency peaks during summer and holiday periods, while winter sees a modest reduction. Overall, travellers have a wide range of departure times to choose from each day.

What is the flight distance between New York and London?

The great‑circle distance between JFK and Heathrow is approximately 5,540 km (3,440 mi). This figure represents the shortest path over the Earth's surface and is the basis for most flight‑time calculations. Actual flight routes may deviate slightly due to air‑traffic control and weather, but the distance remains a useful benchmark.

What is the best time of year to visit London, and what is the weather like?

Late spring (May) and early autumn (September) offer mild temperatures ranging from 12 °C to 18 °C (54 °F to 64 °F) and lower rainfall compared to winter. These periods also see fewer crowds at major attractions. Summer can be warm and humid, while December and January are cooler, with temperatures often falling below 5 °C (41 °F) and frequent rain.

How do I get the cheapest flight from New York to London?

Use a fare‑comparison engine to compare all airlines and dates, and set up price alerts for your preferred route. Be flexible with departure and return dates, and consider flying mid‑week or at off‑peak times. Booking 6–8 weeks in advance usually yields the best prices, and choosing a carrier with a low‑cost base or a partner alliance can further reduce costs. Avoiding extra fees by packing within the free allowance also helps.

Which is the cheapest airline on this route, and what is the trade‑off?

Among the main carriers, Virgin Atlantic often offers the lowest economy fares, especially when promotional deals are available. The trade‑off is a slightly smaller seat pitch and fewer complimentary amenities compared to British Airways or Delta. Low‑cost options such as Norwegian were previously available but have ceased trans‑Atlantic services, so most travellers rely on the standard carriers for the best value.

What is the most popular connecting hub for flights from New York to London?

While most flights are direct, many passengers use European hubs such as Paris‑Charles de Gaulle, Frankfurt, or Dublin to connect to London. Among these, Paris‑Charles de Gaulle is the most frequented, offering a high frequency of flights to Heathrow and Gatwick and a wide range of onward connections across the UK. The layover times are typically 1.5 to 3 hours, making it a convenient transfer point.
