Overview of Flying the Route

A non‑stop flight from New York’s John F. Kennedy International Airport to London Heathrow takes roughly 6 hours 30 minutes, give or take depending on wind and the exact departure time. The great circle distance is 5 540 km, so the average cruise speed is around 860 km/h.

British Airways and Virgin Atlantic dominate the route with several daily departures. American Airlines, Delta Air Lines, United Airlines and JetBlue Airways also operate non‑stop services, usually in the early‑morning or late‑evening slots. All carriers offer a full range of cabin classes and loyalty programmes.

The route is one of the busiest trans‑Atlantic corridors, with roughly 45 non‑stop departures from JFK to LHR each week. Most days of the week you will find at least two or three options that depart in the early morning, midday or late evening, giving travellers good flexibility.

When booking, it pays to compare fares across all carriers on flexible dates and to set price alerts. Booking 6–8 weeks in advance often yields the lowest prices, especially if you avoid the weekend surge and the peak summer and Christmas periods.

Airports in New York and Getting There

John F. Kennedy International Airport is one of the world’s busiest hubs, with six terminals spread across a 2 km stretch of runway. Terminals 4 and 5 are the main international terminals for flights to London, while Terminal 7 is used by some low‑cost carriers.

Getting to JFK from Manhattan is straightforward. The AirTrain links the airport to the Long Island Railway (LIRR) at Jamaica and Howard Beach stations, which then connect to the NYC Subway system. A taxi or ride‑share from midtown costs around $70–$90, while the LIRR ticket from Manhattan is about $10.30 one way.

If you live in the outer boroughs or on Long Island, the LIRR or a bus route such as the 128 or 135 to the airport offer convenient alternatives. For those with a lot of luggage, a private shuttle or car‑service can provide a door‑to‑door experience.

Airports in London and Getting There

Heathrow Airport (LHR) is the UK’s main international gateway, located 23 km west of central London. It has four terminals, with Terminals 2–4 handling most long‑haul flights to New York and Terminal 5 serving a smaller number of trans‑Atlantic services.

The Heathrow Express offers a 15‑minute journey to Paddington Station, while the Elizabeth Line (formerly Piccadilly Line) provides a 45‑minute link to the city centre. National Rail services from Heathrow Terminals 2–4 to London Paddington and Terminal 5 to London Paddington are also available, as are the London Underground Piccadilly Line and numerous bus routes.

If you are looking to save on airport fees, Gatwick (46 km south) and Stansted (64 km north) are alternative hubs that receive a high volume of flights from New York. Both airports are served by the Gatwick Express, Stansted Express and a range of coach operators that connect directly to central London.

Finding the Cheapest Fares

Start by using a fare‑comparison engine that aggregates prices from all major carriers. Many sites allow you to view a calendar of the cheapest days over a month and to set price alerts.

Travellers who can shift their travel dates by a day or two often find that Tuesday and Wednesday mornings are the cheapest, especially during the low‑season period from late October to early March.

Flying into Gatwick or Stansted instead of Heathrow can reduce the airport fee by £20–£40, and some airlines offer slightly lower base fares on those hubs. However, you should factor in the extra travel time to central London when calculating total cost.

If you need a business‑class seat, book early – premium fares peak 4–6 weeks before departure. For economy, 6–8 weeks ahead usually locks in a good price, but keep an eye on flash sales or airline‑specific promotions that can shave £50–£100 off a ticket.

Best Time of Year to Travel

The low‑season window from late October to early March offers the most attractive fares, as business travel slows and the summer and holiday rushes have ended. Peak season – June to August – sees higher prices and larger crowds, but the weather is warmest.

London’s climate is temperate, with mild winters and cool summers. The wettest months are typically October to January, while July and August can be warm but still damp. Late spring (April to early June) and early autumn (September to early October) provide comfortable temperatures and fewer tourists.

If you enjoy cultural events, late spring hosts the Wimbledon tennis championships and the Royal Festival Hall season, while early autumn features the London Film Festival and the New Year’s Eve fireworks at the Thames.

Book 2–3 months in advance for the best fares, and consider booking 6–8 weeks ahead if you need a business‑class seat. Avoid the first week of December and the week before New Year, when prices spike.

Getting Around London and Highlights

London’s public‑transport network is world‑class. The Tube (London Underground) covers most of the city, with the Central Line and Northern Line providing quick links to Heathrow and other airports. The Elizabeth Line (Crossrail) offers fast east‑west connections, while buses and river ferries provide scenic alternatives.

Key sights include the British Museum, the Tower of London, Buckingham Palace, the Houses of Parliament and the Tate Modern. For a panoramic view, head to the London Eye or the Shard. A day‑pass for the Tube and buses gives you unlimited travel for 24 hours, which is a cost‑effective way to explore.

If you have limited time, consider a hop‑on hop‑off bus tour that covers the main attractions and gives you a quick overview of the city. For a more relaxed pace, book a guided walking tour that takes you through historic streets and hidden gems.

Which airports serve London (and New York), and how far are they from the city centre?

London is served by Heathrow (LHR), Gatwick (LGW), Stansted (STN) and Luton (LTN). Heathrow is about 23 km (14 mi) from central London, reachable by the Piccadilly Line, Heathrow Express or road. New York’s main international gateway is JFK, roughly 12 km (7 mi) from Manhattan, with access via the AirTrain, subway, or taxi. Newark (EWR) and LaGuardia (LGA) are additional U.S. airports for the New York area.

How many flights operate between New York and London, and what is the distance?

The New York‑London corridor sees around 40–50 daily flights, including non‑stop and one‑stop services. The great‑circle distance between JFK and LHR is approximately 5,540 km (3,445 mi). This high traffic volume keeps competition strong and fares relatively competitive.

What is the best time of year to visit London, and what is the weather like?

Late spring (May to early June) and early autumn (September to early October) offer mild temperatures, around 15–20 °C, and fewer crowds. Rain is moderate year‑round, but these months have the best balance of sunshine and comfortable weather for sightseeing. Avoid July and August when temperatures climb above 25 °C and crowds swell.

Which is the cheapest airline on this route, and what is the trade‑off?

Historically, Norwegian was the cheapest carrier on the New York‑London route, offering fares as low as £200 in economy. The trade‑off includes limited in‑flight services, fewer checked‑bag allowances, and a smaller seat pitch. If you value comfort and baggage, British Airways or Virgin Atlantic provide a higher‑quality experience at a slightly higher price.

What are the main connecting hubs for flights from New York to London?

London Heathrow is the primary hub for trans‑Atlantic flights, but many airlines also use Gatwick, Stansted or Luton for connecting services.
