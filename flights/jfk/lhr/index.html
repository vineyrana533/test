Overview of flying the route

Flying from New York to London is one of the most popular trans‑Atlantic routes, with a large number of daily flights. The journey covers roughly 5 540 km and takes around seven and a half hours on a direct flight. Because of the high demand, airlines offer a range of options from full‑service carriers to low‑cost operators, allowing travellers to choose based on budget, comfort and preferred departure time. The route is served by several airlines that maintain a strong presence on both sides of the Atlantic, ensuring frequent connections and competitive pricing.

The flight is typically operated from John F. Kennedy International Airport (JFK), one of the world’s busiest hubs, which offers extensive facilities and a wide range of airline choices. Heathrow Airport (LHR) is the main London gateway, handling the majority of international traffic and providing excellent rail and road connections to the city centre. The combination of JFK and LHR gives travellers a high level of convenience, with multiple daily departures and arrivals that fit most itineraries.

Because the route is heavily trafficked, you can often find flights that depart at almost any time of day, from early morning to late night. This flexibility allows you to plan around your schedule, whether you prefer a morning arrival to maximise sightseeing or a late‑night flight to avoid crowds at the airport. Additionally, the competition between airlines on this corridor keeps fares relatively affordable compared to less frequent routes.

Airports serving New York

John F. Kennedy International Airport (JFK) is the primary gateway for trans‑Atlantic flights from New York. Located about 24 km southeast of Manhattan, it is served by the AirTrain and a network of taxis, rideshares and bus services. The airport’s six terminals are dedicated to different airlines, with Terminal 1 and 2 mainly for American and Delta, Terminal 4 for United, Terminal 5 for international carriers, Terminal 7 for Virgin Atlantic, and Terminal 8 for other operators. The extensive public transport links mean you can reach the airport from Manhattan in 45–60 minutes depending on traffic.

In addition to JFK, New York City has other airports that can be considered for international travel. La Guardia (LGA) lies just 8 km northeast of Manhattan and offers limited international services, primarily to the Caribbean and a few European destinations. Newark Liberty International Airport (EWR) is about 30 km south of the city, serving a wide range of airlines and providing convenient access via the AirTrain, trains and buses. For most long‑haul flights to London, however, JFK remains the most popular choice due to its extensive flight network and dedicated terminal facilities.

If you are looking for a cheaper alternative or a different departure point, Newark is a good option, especially for carriers that operate from there. However, the travel time to the airport and the number of direct flights to London are slightly lower than at JFK. For travellers prioritising convenience and a higher frequency of flights, JFK is typically the preferred airport.

Airports serving London

Heathrow Airport (LHR) is the busiest airport in the United Kingdom and the primary arrival point for flights from New York. Located 23 km west of central London, Heathrow is served by the Heathrow Express, which takes 15 minutes to reach Paddington Station, and by the Piccadilly Line of the Underground, offering a convenient and affordable link to the city. The airport has four terminals, with Terminal 5 handling most long‑haul flights and Terminal 4 used by many low‑cost carriers.

Other London airports that can be used for international travel include Gatwick (LGW), Stansted (STN), Luton (LTN) and City Airport (LCY). Gatwick, situated 46 km south of the city, is a popular alternative for budget airlines and offers the Gatwick Express, which takes 30 minutes to Victoria. Stansted and Luton are further out, approximately 60 km and 45 km from central London, and are served by regional rail and coach services. City Airport is the closest, just 12 km east of the City of London, but it mainly handles business and low‑cost flights.

While all these airports provide access to London, Heathrow remains the most convenient for most New York travellers because of its direct rail link, extensive transportation options and the high frequency of flights from the United States. If you are willing to travel a bit further by train or coach, Gatwick can offer lower fares and a less busy arrival experience.

How to find the cheapest fares

The best way to locate the lowest fares on the JFK–LHR route is to use a dedicated flight‑comparison engine that aggregates prices from multiple airlines and travel agencies. Set up price alerts for your preferred travel dates; this will notify you when fares drop or when a special promotion becomes available. Booking 6–8 weeks in advance usually yields the lowest prices, as airlines tend to raise fares as the departure date approaches.

Avoid peak periods such as the summer holidays, Christmas and New Year, as fares can be 20–30 % higher during these times. Mid‑week departures, especially on Tuesdays and Wednesdays, are typically cheaper than weekend flights. Early‑morning or late‑night flights can also offer lower fares because they are less in demand.

Consider flying into Gatwick if you are flexible with your arrival airport; fares to Gatwick are often cheaper than Heathrow, and the Gatwick Express provides a quick link to central London. Using airline miles or credit‑card points can further reduce the out‑of‑pocket cost, particularly on carriers that accept frequent‑flyer programmes. Finally, keep an eye on seasonal promotions from airlines such as Virgin Atlantic and British Airways, which sometimes offer discounted fares for a limited time.

Best time of year to travel

The most affordable months for the JFK–London route are September to November, when business travel falls and leisure demand is moderate. Prices are usually lowest in the first half of the month, especially on mid‑week days, and tend to rise again toward the end of September and into October. If you prefer a cooler climate and fewer crowds, late spring (April–May) and early autumn (September–October) are ideal, with mild temperatures and plenty of cultural events.

Summer months (June–August) are the peak season for trans‑Atlantic travel, with higher fares and larger crowds at both airports. However, the weather in London during this period is generally warm and pleasant, with average temperatures between 18 °C and 22 °C. Winter (December–February) offers lower fares but cooler temperatures, ranging from 2 °C to 8 °C, and the possibility of rain or snow.

If you are traveling for a specific event or holiday, such as the London Marathon in April or the Christmas markets in December, plan your trip early and book well in advance. For the most flexible itinerary, aim for mid‑week departures and avoid public holidays in both the United States and the United Kingdom.

About London – getting around and highlights

London is a vast, multi‑layered city that offers a wealth of attractions. The city’s public transport network, including the Underground, Overground, buses and river services, provides convenient access to most major sights. A single Oyster card or contact‑less payment card can be used across all modes of transport, making it easy to travel between the airport, the city centre and tourist destinations.

Key highlights for visitors include the historic Westminster area, with Buckingham Palace, the Houses of Parliament and Big Ben; the cultural hub of the South Bank, home to the Tate Modern, Shakespeare’s Globe and the London Eye; and the historic Tower of London and Tower Bridge. For a taste of contemporary art, visit the Saatchi Gallery or the Victoria and Albert Museum. If time allows, a day trip to Windsor Castle or Oxford is also popular.

London’s diverse neighbourhoods, such as Camden, Notting Hill, Shoreditch and Greenwich, offer distinct atmospheres, markets, restaurants and street art. Exploring these areas on foot or by bike can give you a deeper understanding of the city’s character. Finally, consider purchasing a London Pass or a travel card for discounted entry to many attractions and unlimited travel on public transport during your stay.

How much does a flight from New York JFK to London Heathrow usually cost / what drives the price?

Prices for a one‑way flight from New York JFK to London Heathrow normally range from about £250 to £600 (≈US$300–$700) in economy, depending on the carrier, booking window, and season. Peak periods such as Christmas, Easter, and late summer can push fares above £700. Low‑cost carriers keep base fares lower but charge extra for checked baggage, seat selection, and onboard meals. Fuel price fluctuations, currency exchange rates, and airline demand also influence the final ticket cost.

What is the best day of the week and time of day to fly this route for a lower fare?

The cheapest day of the week to fly from New York JFK to London Heathrow is usually Tuesday or Wednesday, when airlines offer the lowest fares. Early‑morning departures (around 5:00–6:00 am) can also be cheaper because they are less popular. Late‑night flights after 10:00 pm tend to be more expensive due to lower demand. Which airports serve New York and London, and how far are they from the city centre?

New York is served by John F. Kennedy International Airport (JFK), located about 13 km (8 mi) southeast of Manhattan’s Midtown. London’s main international hub is Heathrow Airport (LHR), situated roughly 23 km (14 mi) west of central London. From JFK, the city centre can be reached by AirTrain to Jamaica Station and the 7 train, or by taxi and rideshare, taking 45–60 minutes depending on traffic. From Heathrow, the Piccadilly Line of the London Underground, the Heathrow Express, or taxis take 45–60 minutes to reach central London.

How many flights operate between New York JFK and London Heathrow, and what is the distance?

There are roughly 20 direct flights per day between New York JFK and London Heathrow, operated by carriers such as British Airways, Virgin Atlantic, American Airlines, Delta, United, Air France, Lufthansa, and Icelandair. The great‑circle distance between the two airports is about 5 540 km (3 450 mi). With a typical non‑stop flight time of 7 hours 30 minutes, the route is one of the busiest trans‑Atlantic corridors in the world.

What is the best time of year to visit London, and what is the weather like?

The best time of year to visit London is spring (April and May) and early autumn (September and October). During these months the weather is mild, with average temperatures between 11 °C and 18 °C (52 °F to 64 °F). Rain is moderate, and the city’s parks and museums are at their most accessible. Visiting in spring or early autumn also avoids the peak tourist crowds of July–August.

Which is the cheapest airline on this route, and what is the trade‑off?

On this route, the cheapest airline is typically a low‑cost carrier such as Norwegian (when operating) or a sale from a full‑service airline like British Airways. The trade‑off is that low‑fare tickets usually exclude checked baggage, seat selection, and onboard meals, so passengers pay extra for these services. Full‑service carriers offer a more inclusive experience but at a higher base price. Choosing between them depends on how much extra baggage or comfort you need.
