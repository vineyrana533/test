Overview of the New York to London flight

The route from John F. Kennedy International Airport to Heathrow covers approximately 5,540 kilometres and is one of the busiest trans‑Atlantic corridors. Most carriers operate direct services that take between 7 and 8 hours, depending on wind conditions. The flight leaves New York in the late afternoon or early evening, arriving in London the next morning in the early local time, which is convenient for business travellers and tourists alike.

Passengers can expect a range of cabin classes from economy to first. The airlines offer in‑flight entertainment, Wi‑Fi and meal services that vary by carrier and aircraft type. The consistent high traffic means that airlines keep the schedule tight, with most flights departing within a 30‑minute window of each other.

Because the route is heavily trafficked, competition keeps fares relatively stable, and many airlines offer frequent‑flyer benefits for repeat travellers.

Getting to and from JFK in New York

John F. Kennedy International Airport is located about 24 kilometres southeast of Manhattan. It is served by three main terminals (A, B, and C) and the dedicated Terminal 4 for low‑cost carriers. The airport is connected to the city via the AirTrain, which links to the Long Island Rail Road and the New York City Subway at Jamaica Station.

The AirTrain is a convenient option for most travellers, with a flat fare of £4.75 (US$6.50) and a journey to Manhattan taking roughly 45 minutes. Taxis, rideshare services and private shuttles are also available, with fares ranging from £30 to £50 (US$40–70) depending on traffic.

If you are staying in the boroughs of Queens or Brooklyn, the LIRR can be a cost‑effective alternative, especially when combined with a MetroCard for the subway.

Getting to and from Heathrow Airport

Heathrow Airport is situated 23 kilometres west of central London. It comprises four terminals (1, 2, 3 and 4) and is the world’s busiest international airport. The primary public transport link is the Heathrow Express, a 15‑minute train that runs every 15 minutes to Paddington Station for a fare of £22 (US$30). For a cheaper option, the TfL Rail (now part of the Elizabeth Line) takes about 45 minutes to Paddington and costs £25 (US$34).

The Underground’s Piccadilly Line also serves Heathrow, with a journey to Leicester Square taking roughly 60 minutes and a fare of £6 (US$8). Buses and taxis are plentiful, with a typical taxi fare to central London ranging from £45 to £60 (US$60–80) during the day.

Passengers arriving at Heathrow should be aware of the 24‑hour baggage claim and the need to pass through UK border control before accessing the city transport links.

Finding the cheapest fares on the JFK–LHR route

Because the route is heavily trafficked, price comparison tools are essential. Start by checking the airlines’ own websites for any promotional fares, then compare with aggregators that show a range of options from budget carriers to full‑service airlines.

Flexibility with dates is key; mid‑week departures are often cheaper than weekend flights. Booking 6 to 8 weeks in advance typically yields the best prices, with last‑minute deals becoming increasingly rare as the departure date approaches.

Consider alternative flight paths: a one‑stop flight via a hub such as Dubai or Doha can sometimes offer lower fares, especially if you are willing to trade a longer travel time for a cheaper ticket. Always check the total travel time and layover duration before committing.

Best time of year to travel and what to expect

London’s climate is temperate, with mild winters and warm summers. The peak tourist season runs from late spring to early autumn (May to September), when temperatures average 18–22 °C and daylight hours are longest. During this period, airport and city transport can be busy, and accommodation prices rise.

The low season, from October to March, offers cooler temperatures (5–10 °C) and fewer crowds. Flights are usually cheaper, but some attractions may have reduced opening hours. The winter months also bring a festive atmosphere, with Christmas lights and markets.

If you prefer a balance between pleasant weather and moderate prices, late spring (April) and early autumn (September) are ideal. Avoid the peak holiday periods (late December to early January) if cost is a priority.

Getting around London and must‑see highlights

London’s public transport network is extensive. The Underground (Tube) covers most of the city, and the Oyster card or contact‑less payment offers discounted fares. For longer journeys, the National Rail network connects central London to suburbs and nearby towns.

Key attractions include the British Museum, the Tower of London, Buckingham Palace and the Tate Modern. The city’s riverside offers a scenic walk along the Thames, with options to take a riverboat to Greenwich or the Tower Bridge.

For a taste of local culture, explore the markets of Camden, the street art of Shoreditch, or the historic pubs in the City of London. Each neighbourhood offers a distinct atmosphere, from the cosmopolitan vibes of Soho to the historic charm of Westminster.

Finding cheap flights from New York to London: frequently asked questions

How do I find cheap flights from New York JFK to London Heathrow?

To find cheap flights from New York JFK to London Heathrow, search for flexible dates, book 1‑3 months ahead, and look for the off‑peak season between late September and November. Avoid peak summer and Christmas periods. Consider nearby airports such as Newark (EWR) or LaGuardia (LGA) for lower fares, and use this page to compare prices, set alerts, and monitor changes.

What is the cheapest way to fly from New York JFK to London Heathrow?

The cheapest way to fly from New York JFK to London Heathrow is to book a low‑cost carrier such as Virgin Atlantic or use a discount fare on a full‑service airline during off‑peak times. Look for flights that depart late at night or early in the morning, and book 2‑3 months in advance. This route is also cheaper if you fly out of Newark (EWR) and return to JFK, as the combined fare can be lower.

When is the best time to book a flight from New York JFK to London Heathrow to get the lowest price?

The best time to book a flight from New York JFK to London Heathrow for the lowest price is 1‑3 months before departure, usually between early October and early December. Booking during this window captures the early‑bird discount and avoids the price surge that occurs a few weeks before the flight. Monitoring fares on this page and setting alerts can help you spot a good deal as soon as it appears.

What is the cheapest month or time of year to fly from New York JFK to London Heathrow?

The cheapest month to fly from New York JFK to London Heathrow is late September to early November, with the lowest fares typically in late October. The next best period is early March to early April, when demand is lower after the winter holidays. Prices rise sharply in July, August, and during the Christmas and New Year periods.

How long is the flight from New York JFK to London Heathrow?

The flight from New York JFK to London Heathrow takes approximately 7 hours 15 minutes on a direct flight, covering a distance of about 5 540 km. Layovers add 2 hours to 4 hours of additional time, depending on the carrier and routing.

Which airlines fly from New York JFK to London Heathrow?

Airlines that operate direct flights from New York JFK to London Heathrow include British Airways, Virgin Atlantic, American Airlines, Delta Air Lines, United Airlines, and occasionally Air France via a short stop in Paris. Low‑cost carriers such as Norwegian used to serve the route but are no longer operating direct flights. All of these airlines offer multiple daily departures, with British Airways and Virgin Atlantic providing the most frequent service.

Are there direct (non‑stop) flights from New York JFK to London Heathrow?

Yes, there are direct (non‑stop) flights from New York JFK to London Heathrow. British Airways, Virgin Atlantic, American Airlines, Delta Air Lines and United Airlines all operate daily non‑stop services between the two airports.

How much does a flight from New York JFK to London Heathrow usually cost and what drives the price?

A round‑trip flight from New York JFK to London Heathrow normally costs between £350 and £650, depending on the airline, time of year, and how far in advance it is booked. Prices rise during peak summer, Christmas, and early spring, while off‑peak periods such as late September to early November offer the lowest fares. Factors such as seat class, baggage allowance, and flight timing also influence the final cost.

What is the best day of the week and time of day to fly this route for a lower fare?

The cheapest day of the week to fly from New York JFK to London Heathrow is usually Tuesday or Wednesday, when airlines lower fares to fill seats. Early‑morning departures (before 6 am) or late‑night flights (after 10 pm) also tend to be cheaper because they attract fewer passengers.

Which airports serve London and New York, and how far are they from the city centre?

New York’s main international gateway is John F. Kennedy International Airport (JFK), located about 13 km (8 mi) southeast of Manhattan’s core. London is served by Heathrow Airport (LHR), which sits roughly 23 km (14 mi) west of central London. Both airports have extensive public‑transport links: the AirTrain and NYC‑subway connect JFK to Manhattan, while the Heathrow Express, Piccadilly Line and bus services link LHR to the city centre.

How many flights operate between New York JFK and London Heathrow, and what is the distance?

Approximately 30 direct flights operate daily between New York JFK and London Heathrow, covering a distance of 5 540 km (3 443 mi). The frequency varies by carrier, but British Airways and Virgin Atlantic typically run 4–5 flights each day in both directions, while American, Delta and United provide 2–3 daily services.

What is the best time of year to visit London, and what is the weather like?

The best time of year to visit London is late spring (April and May) or early autumn (September and October), when temperatures range from 12 °C to 18 °C and rainfall is moderate. Summer (June–August) is warmer but can be crowded, while winter (December–February) is cold and often wet. Spring offers the most pleasant weather for sightseeing.

Which is the cheapest airline on this route, and what is the trade‑off?

Virgin Atlantic is generally the cheapest airline on the New York JFK to London Heathrow route, offering lower base fares than British Airways. The trade‑off is a more limited in‑flight service and fewer lounge options. British Airways provides a higher level of comfort, complimentary meals and priority boarding, but at a higher price point.

What is the best way to get from Heathrow to central London?

The quickest way to travel from Heathrow Airport to central London is the Heathrow Express, which takes 15 minutes to Paddington and costs about £25 one‑way. Alternatively, the Piccadilly Line of the London Underground runs every 10 minutes and costs £6, while a bus or taxi offers more flexibility but higher cost.
