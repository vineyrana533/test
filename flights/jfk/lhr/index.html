Overview of the route

The flight from New York’s John F. Kennedy International Airport to London Heathrow covers a distance of approximately 5,540 kilometres. Direct services normally take between seven and eight hours, with a five‑hour time zone difference that can make the first part of the journey feel longer. The route is one of the busiest transatlantic corridors, offering a range of carriers and flight frequencies that give travellers flexibility. Whether you choose a morning departure to arrive in the evening or a late‑night flight to maximise daylight in the city, the journey is well supported by a variety of airlines and service levels.

New York airports and ground transport

John F. Kennedy International Airport is served by six terminals, with Terminal 4 and 5 handling most international flights. The airport is linked to Manhattan by the AirTrain, which connects to the New York City Subway and Long Island Rail Road. Buses, taxis and rideshare services are also available, as are car‑hire desks throughout the terminals. For those staying in midtown or the boroughs, a combination of the AirTrain and the subway can bring you to the city centre in roughly 45 minutes, depending on traffic.

London Heathrow and local transport

Heathrow Airport has four passenger terminals, with Terminals 2, 3, 4 and 5 handling the bulk of international traffic. The airport is situated about 23 kilometres west of central London. The fastest link to the city is the Heathrow Express train, which reaches Paddington in 15 minutes. The Piccadilly Line of the London Underground also serves the airport, offering a cheaper alternative that takes about 45 minutes to reach central London. Buses, taxis and private car hire are plentiful, and a range of parking options are available for those who prefer to drive.

Finding the cheapest fares

To secure the best price, compare fares across several search engines and set price alerts for the New York to London corridor. Flexibility with departure dates can yield significant savings, especially when avoiding weekends and peak holiday periods. Booking six to eight weeks in advance often produces the lowest fares, although last‑minute deals can occasionally appear if airlines are looking to fill seats. Consider flying into nearby airports such as Gatwick or Stansted, which sometimes offer cheaper alternatives, but be mindful of the additional ground‑transport cost to central London.

Best time of year to travel

The low season for transatlantic flights typically runs from late January to March, when demand is lower and airlines offer promotional fares. Mid‑summer and the Christmas/New Year period represent peak season, with higher prices and fuller flights. Weather in London is mild in spring and early autumn, making these periods ideal for sightseeing and outdoor activities. If you prefer cooler temperatures and fewer crowds, late autumn can also be a pleasant time to visit, though rain is more frequent.

Getting around London and key sights

London offers an extensive public‑transport network that includes the Underground, buses, trams and river services. A Visitor Oyster card or contactless payment card gives you convenient access to most modes of transport. Major attractions such as Buckingham Palace, the Tower of London and the British Museum are all within the central zone and easily reachable by tube or bus. Museums in the South Bank and the National Gallery in Trafalgar Square are free to enter, while iconic landmarks like the London Eye and St. Paul’s Cathedral offer a mix of free and paid experiences.

Finding cheap flights from New York to London: frequently asked questions

How long is the flight from New York to London?

New York JFK to London Heathrow takes roughly 7 hours and 15 minutes to 8 hours, depending on wind and routing. The aircraft typically flies a great‑circle path of about 5,540 km. Commercial jets such as the Boeing 777 or Airbus A350 are common, and the flight usually departs in the evening from New York to arrive in London early morning. Weather in the North Atlantic can add a few minutes, but most flights stay within the 7‑hour window.

Which airlines fly from New York JFK to London Heathrow?

Airlines that fly nonstop from New York JFK to London Heathrow include British Airways, Virgin Atlantic, American Airlines, Delta Air Lines, United Airlines, Air France, Lufthansa, and Scandinavian Airlines. Low‑cost carriers such as Norwegian (when operating) and easyJet occasionally offer trans‑Atlantic services, but the main carriers are the four U.S. majors and the two British flag carriers. All of them operate wide‑body jets and provide a mix of economy, premium economy, business and first‑class cabins.

Are there direct (non‑stop) flights from New York to London?

Yes, there are nonstop flights from New York JFK to London Heathrow. British Airways, Virgin Atlantic, American Airlines, Delta, United and other carriers operate direct services on this route. The flight is usually scheduled for the evening in New York, arriving in London early the next morning, and the opposite direction departs London in the afternoon to arrive in New York the following evening.

How much does a flight from New York to London cost / what drives the price?

Prices for a round‑trip from New York JFK to London Heathrow typically range from about £200 to £600 in economy, depending on how far in advance you book, the day of the week, and the season. Peak summer and Christmas periods push fares above £600, while off‑peak winter and shoulder months can see lower rates. Factors such as baggage fees, seat selection, and the choice of carrier also influence the final cost.

What is the cheapest month to fly from New York to London?

The cheapest month to fly from New York to London is usually late September through November, when demand drops after the summer rush and before the Christmas peak. Early October often offers the best balance of lower fares and mild weather. Booking in the middle of the month can also secure better deals, as airlines adjust pricing after the holiday season ends.

What is the best day of the week and time of day to fly this route for a lower fare?

Midweek flights—particularly Tuesdays and Wednesdays—tend to be cheaper on the New York–London route. Early‑morning departures from JFK around 6 am or 7 am arrive in Heathrow just after sunrise, and airlines often price these slots lower. Conversely, weekend flights and late‑night departures usually carry a premium. Checking fares for 6:00–8:00 am or 7:00–9:00 pm windows can reveal the best bargains.

How far in advance should I book flights from New York to London?

Booking 6 to 8 weeks in advance is usually optimal for the New York–London route. Prices tend to rise as the departure date approaches, especially during peak seasons. However, last‑minute deals can appear during off‑peak periods, so monitoring fares for a few days before departure can also uncover savings. Setting up price alerts on comparison sites helps capture sudden drops.

Which airports serve London (and New York), and how far are they from the city centre?

New York is served by John F. Kennedy International Airport (JFK), located about 13 km (8 mi) southeast of Manhattan’s city centre, and also by La Guardia (LGA) and Newark (EWR) which are farther away. London’s main international hub is Heathrow Airport (LHR), situated roughly 15 km (9 mi) west of central London. Both airports have extensive public transport links: the AirTrain and subway from JFK, and the Heathrow Express or Underground from Heathrow, making city access convenient.

How many flights operate between New York and London?

There are usually between 20 and 25 nonstop flights per day between New York JFK and London Heathrow, operated by the major carriers. The schedule is dense, with departures from JFK every hour or two from 6 am to midnight, and Heathrow arrivals spread across the 24‑hour day. During peak summer and holiday periods, the frequency can rise to 30 or more flights, while off‑peak weeks see a few fewer services.

What is the flight distance between New York and London?

The great‑circle distance between New York JFK and London Heathrow is about 5,540 km (3,440 miles). This is the shortest path over the earth’s surface and is what airlines use to calculate fuel and flight time. The actual flight path may be slightly longer due to air‑traffic routing and weather, but the 5,540 km figure is the standard reference.

What is the best time of year to visit London, and what is the weather like?

London is best visited in late spring (May and June) or early autumn (September and October) when temperatures are mild, ranging from 12 °C to 20 °C, and rain is moderate. These seasons offer comfortable walking weather and fewer crowds than summer. Winter can be cold and wet, while summer is hot and busy. The city’s cultural calendar peaks in spring with events like the Chelsea Flower Show and in autumn with the London Film Festival.

How do I get the cheapest flight from New York to London?

To secure the cheapest flight from New York to London, start by comparing fares on multiple comparison sites and set price alerts. Book 6–8 weeks ahead and look for Tuesday or Wednesday departures in the early‑morning slot. Use flexible dates to spot lower fares, and consider airlines that offer free checked baggage, such as British Airways or Virgin Atlantic, to avoid extra fees. Checking the airline’s own website can sometimes reveal exclusive promotions.

Which is the cheapest airline on this route, and what is the trade‑off?

Virgin Atlantic and British Airways are typically the most affordable carriers on the New York–London route, especially when booking in advance. Their fares include a checked bag, which is a significant saving compared to low‑cost carriers that charge extra for baggage. The trade‑off is slightly higher seat pitch in economy and a longer turnaround time at JFK, but the overall value is often better than the cheapest fares from airlines that charge for every amenity.

What is a major connecting hub on the New York–London route?

Heathrow Airport is a major international hub and a key connecting point for flights between the United States and Europe. From Heathrow, passengers can transfer to numerous European destinations via British Airways, Virgin Atlantic, and other carriers. The airport’s extensive rail and underground links, including the Heathrow Express and Piccadilly Line, make it a convenient gateway for onward travel across the UK and mainland Europe.
